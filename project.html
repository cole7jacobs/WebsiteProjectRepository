Engagement Context & Simulation Notice

This project was a team-based simulated client engagement completed as part of internship training during my Technology Risk internship at Ernst & Young (EY). All client scenarios, walkthroughs, and documentation requests were conducted with a mock federal agency for educational and skill-development purposes. This case study reflects academic learning and does not represent an actual federal client engagement, proprietary client data, or internal EY methodology.

Project Overview

Context, structure, and objectives of the simulated technology risk engagement.

During my Technology Risk internship at EY, I participated in a team-based simulated client engagement in which our client was a mock federal agency. The project was designed to give us experience working through an IT risk and controls assessment in a setting similar to a federal technology risk engagement.

Our team was responsible for understanding the agency's IT processes and controls, conducting a mock controls walkthrough, communicating with the client, requesting supporting documentation, and reviewing the evidence provided.

We used the NIST SP 800-53 framework to help guide our understanding and assessment of the agency's IT controls.

The Process

The six progressive stages followed throughout the simulated assessment lifecycle.

01

Understand the Control

Reviewed the IT control being assessed and used NIST SP 800-53 to understand the relevant risk and control considerations.

02

Conduct the Walkthrough

Participated in a simulated walkthrough with the federal-agency client to better understand how its IT processes and controls operated.

03

Ask Questions

Asked questions during the walkthrough to clarify the process, understand how the control was performed, and identify what supporting evidence would be needed.

04

Request Documentation

Communicated with the client to explain what documentation and evidence our team needed to support the assessment.

05

Review Evidence

Reviewed the documentation provided by the client and connected the evidence back to the control being assessed.

06

Collaborate as a Team

Worked with my team throughout the simulation to discuss the walkthrough, documentation, controls, and our understanding of the client's processes.

What I Learned

Reflections on risk analysis, client collaboration, and consultative skills.

This project helped me understand that IT risk work involves much more than simply knowing a framework or looking at documentation. I learned the importance of understanding why a control exists, asking clear and useful questions during a walkthrough, knowing what evidence to request, and being able to clearly explain those requests to a client.

Working through the simulated federal-agency engagement also helped me see the connection between technical concepts such as NIST SP 800-53 and IT controls and professional skills such as communication, teamwork, and working with a client.

Understanding the "Why" Behind Controls

Controls are more than compliance checklists. Real risk assessment requires grasping the operational risk behind the system and knowing how to evaluate whether procedures truly mitigate vulnerabilities.

Consultative Communication & Teamwork

Asking focused walkthrough questions and articulating exactly why specific evidence is needed builds transparency and smooth collaboration between consultants and client stakeholders.
